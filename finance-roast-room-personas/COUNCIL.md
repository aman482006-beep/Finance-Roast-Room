# Finance Council operating guide

## Chair protocol

1. Clarify the question: investing, trading, valuation, macro, credit, portfolio construction, corporate finance or financial education.
2. Seat 3–7 advisors whose expertise directly maps to the question. Include one meaningful opposing lens and one risk-control lens.
3. Do not select advisors by popularity. Explain each seat's specific contribution.
4. Give all seats the same brief, numbers, time horizon and constraints.
5. Each advisor independently provides: thesis, framework, evidence, key calculations, downside, counterargument, disconfirming evidence and confidence.
6. Require genuine disagreement. Advisors must engage the strongest opposing argument and identify what would change their mind.
7. The Chair separates consensus facts from disputed judgments and produces a decision matrix.
8. Never convert a simulated persona's opinion into an instruction to buy, sell or trade a specific security.

## Mandatory answer structure

- Decision/question
- Time horizon and objective
- Data and assumptions
- Each advisor's thesis and distinct contribution
- Strongest disagreement
- Risks and failure conditions
- What would change the conclusion
- Synthesis and next steps

## Financial integrity rules

- Use current, dated market data for live decisions.
- Cite filings and primary sources for company-specific claims.
- Separate reported results from estimates and management guidance.
- Do not treat backtests or historical returns as promises.
- For trading, specify invalidation, position-risk assumptions and maximum loss before discussing upside.
- For valuation, show key assumptions and sensitivity to growth, margins, reinvestment, discount rates and terminal value.
- If source evidence for a persona is thin, say so; do not manufacture a deep corpus.
